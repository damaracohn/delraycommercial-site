What is my commercial property worth?

Commercial property is valued on the income it produces, not on what nearby buildings sold for. Divide your annual net operating income by the market cap rate and you have the number. A 10-unit building netting $240,000 a year at a 6% cap rate is worth roughly $4 million.

The Core Math

Net operating income divided by cap rate equals value.

That single equation drives almost every commercial sale under $20M in this corridor. Everything else — the condition of the roof, the credit of your tenants, how many years are left on the leases — matters because of how it moves one of those two numbers.

Net operating income (NOI) is what the building actually earns after operating costs, before debt service and before income tax. Cap rate is the return a buyer expects. A lower cap rate means a higher price.

What Moves The Number

Six things that change what a buyer will pay.

Lease structure

Triple net shifts taxes, insurance and maintenance to the tenant. That lifts NOI and usually compresses the cap rate buyers apply.

Remaining lease term

Eight years left on a corporate lease prices very differently from eighteen months. Buyers pay for certainty.

Tenant credit

A national credit tenant and a local operator produce the same rent check until one of them stops. Buyers price that risk.

Insurance

South Florida premiums have reset hard. Insurance sits inside operating expenses, so it comes straight off NOI and off your price.

Deferred maintenance

Roof, structure and systems get deducted from the offer at full estimated cost, usually with a buyer's margin on top.

Zoning and entitlement

If the land underneath is worth more than the building on it, value is set by what can be built, not what is standing.

A Warning

Automated valuations do not work on commercial property.

Zillow, Redfin and the consumer estimate tools are built on residential sales comparables — recent sales of similar houses nearby. Commercial property has no equivalent dataset. There is no centralized MLS for commercial in this corridor, many sales are private, and recorded deed prices often do not separate the real estate from the business, the equipment or the seller financing attached to it.

The practical consequence: an owner who relies on an automated number is usually wrong in one of two expensive directions — pricing so high the building sits and goes stale, or so low they leave six figures on the table and never find out.

Before You Call Anyone

What to have ready.

  • Rent roll — tenants, unit or suite, rent, lease start and end dates.
  • Trailing 12 months of operating expenses — taxes, insurance, management, utilities, repairs.
  • Leases — including amendments, options to renew and any rights of first refusal.
  • Capital work — what has been replaced and when, especially roof, HVAC and structure.
  • Debt — balance, rate, maturity, and whether there is a prepayment penalty.
  • Survey, title policy and any environmental reports — if you have them.

If you do not have all of this, call anyway. Reconstructing it is part of the work, and most owners of long-held buildings do not have a clean file.

Common Questions

Commercial property valuation: common questions

How is commercial property valued?

Primarily by the income approach: annual net operating income divided by the market capitalization rate. Two other methods are used as a cross-check — the sales comparison approach, which looks at comparable commercial sales, and the cost approach, which estimates what it would cost to rebuild. For income-producing property in Palm Beach County, the income approach almost always governs.

What is a cap rate?

The capitalization rate is the annual return a buyer expects, expressed as a percentage of purchase price. A building producing $240,000 of net operating income bought at $4,000,000 is a 6% cap rate. Lower cap rates mean buyers are paying more for each dollar of income.

Why is my commercial property worth less than the building next door?

Usually lease structure or remaining term. Two physically identical buildings can be worth very different amounts if one has long leases with a credit tenant on triple-net terms and the other has short leases with local operators and the landlord paying taxes, insurance and maintenance.

Does a Zillow estimate work for commercial property?

No. Consumer estimate tools are built on residential sales comparables and do not have the income data, lease terms or expense detail that determine commercial value. There is also no centralized commercial MLS in this corridor, so the underlying sales data is incomplete.

Is a broker valuation the same as an appraisal?

No. A broker opinion of value reflects what the market is likely to pay today and is typically free. A certified appraisal is performed by a licensed appraiser, follows USPAP standards, costs money, and is what a lender will require. Many owners get a broker opinion first to decide whether to sell at all.
